October 23, 2026 10 min read
Suppose you’re handed a requirement: ten thousand hours of surveillance footage, and it must answer two kinds of questions. Kind one: “Who is this person,” “where did this car go,” “when did he enter the server room.” Kind two: “What was he doing in there,” “at what moment did he start,” “was he turning those screws fast or slow.” Both kinds are called “visual understanding,” but their fates are completely different. Kind one has been beaten to saturation on public datasets — engineering-wise it’s deliverable today. Kind two is the entire industry’s capability boundary in 2026 — the strongest closed models fail the pass line, and not in a “wait six months” way. This post walks through the state of both, where the boundary sits, what the correct architecture looks like, what the money math is, and where the open-source licensing traps are.
