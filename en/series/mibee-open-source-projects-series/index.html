MiBeeNvr's AI Detection: Why Inference Runs in the Browser, and Why YOLO11n
July 28, 2026 · 10 min read

MiBeeNvr’s Smart Features include an AI detection option that runs inference entirely in the browser — the backend server does no inference at all. The ONNX model file is served to the browser, and ONNX Runtime Web executes it client-side. The model is Ultralytics’ yolo11n.onnx.

This isn’t a common choice among NVR products. Most open-source NVRs (Frigate, the go2rtc ecosystem) put AI inference on the backend, because the backend has access to the raw stream, runs persistently, and can use a GPU. Moving inference into the browser is a deliberate architectural trade-off, not a stopgap. This post breaks down two things: why inference lives in the browser, and — given that premise — why YOLO11n.

MiBeeNvr v0.11.0: fnOS Installs, GB/T 28181, and the Full-Day Timeline
August 17, 2026 · 9 min read

A few days after v0.10.0 went stable, fnOS users’ feedback boiled down to one sentence: it won’t install. Two older debts got settled in the same release: Hikvision/Dahua cameras couldn’t enroll — you either bought their NVR or stood up a separate GB platform; and playback showed one 30-second segment at a time, so reviewing a whole day meant clicking through segment by segment.

It installs now

  • All three install blockers are fixed: stalling on image downloads, a dead desktop icon, and a port field that rejected 9090 outright
  • The offline package bundles the full image set — works the moment it’s installed, no network needed
  • The online package is a few dozen KB and picks whichever of the China mirror or the official registry answers faster
  • The one-click install script auto-selects the same way
  • Synology, QNAP, and ZSpace store templates landed too

Easier to integrate

  • Hikvision/Dahua cameras enroll with a few filled-in fields — no vendor NVR, no separate platform (via the GB/T 28181 national standard)
  • Fully automatic after registration: a camera card per channel, recording starts on its own
  • PTZ, zoom, presets, and voice intercom work; the recordings on the camera’s own SD card are searchable and replayable
  • Hand Home Assistant-style integrations a dedicated key you can revoke on its own, without touching the global password
  • An IP change no longer breaks clients — they track the device, not the address

Playback that works

  • A whole day’s recordings stitch into one timeline — scrub to any moment
  • Segment switches no longer black-flash; the handover is invisible
  • Blank stretches from power cuts scrub right through without stalling
  • Authenticated live streams play on iPhone again

If it doesn’t install, it isn’t released.

Taking Over onvif-go: Real-Camera Fixes, Zero Dependencies, and Service Facades
August 27, 2026 · 9 min read

MiBeeNvr talks to cameras over ONVIF, and this library is what sits underneath. ONVIF is the de facto standard for IP video surveillance equipment: device discovery, media profiles, PTZ control, event subscriptions — all one set of SOAP-over-HTTP service conventions. Any brand that claims ONVIF compliance can, in theory, be integrated through the same calls.

onvif-go is that stack implemented in Go. We did not write it from scratch: its upstream was 0x524a/onvif-go — the previous author reshaped it into its modern form of a client plus a virtual camera server (the git history still traces back to earlier Go ONVIF attempts in the community). But upstream stalled entering 2026: the last merged PR landed in mid-January, the v1.0-through-v1.1.3 release cadence from late 2025 never resumed, and three Improve Test Coverage issues filed in November 2025 sit unclaimed to this day. Meanwhile we hit two problems in our own camera pool that could only be fixed inside the library.

Four Protocol Libraries, Extracted: ONVIF and GB28181 in Go and Rust
August 29, 2026 · 8 min read

MiBee’s camera access runs on two protocols. ONVIF is the de facto standard internationally — Hikvision, Axis, Dahua, and Bosch all speak it. GB/T 28181 is China’s national surveillance standard, unavoidable in government and state-owned projects. The device side (Raspberry Pi camera firmware, one Go and one Rust implementation) and the NVR side (MiBeeNvr, Go) both speak these two protocols, and the stacks have always lived inside the projects.

Two problems with that. First, this code is pickled in interop details — digest-auth qop handling, SIP Via-branch uniqueness, MANSCDP element-vs-attribute dual forms, TCP framing header byte counts — each one was ground out against a real platform, and a new project would have to grind them all again. Second, a library survey two weeks ago showed the open-source landscape for these protocols is unevenly populated; several positions have no one standing in them.

MiBeeNvr v0.12.0: Sub-streams, Adaptive Recording, and Full-path Observability
September 1, 2026 · 18 min read

MiBeeNvr v0.12.0 is out. This release goes after two everyday annoyances: watching the cameras on a phone, where a thumbnail-sized preview quietly pulls the full-resolution video underneath — eating data and heating the phone; and corridor or doorway cameras writing tens of GB a day that nobody ever replays.

Less disk

  • When nothing moves, the camera records less; the moment anything moves, it snaps back to full recording without dropping a frame. Quiet scenes measured at roughly 3/4 less disk
  • A louder sound, a doorbell, or a sensor signal also kicks it back to full recording
  • The replay timeline carries a green-to-red heat strip — the segments where something happened are visible at a glance
  • When the disk nears full, the quietest recordings are deleted first
  • Phone viewing defaults to a small, smooth stream: no stutter, no heat; one tap switches to HD for detail
  • When nobody is watching, that preview stream disconnects by itself — no camera connection or bandwidth tied up
