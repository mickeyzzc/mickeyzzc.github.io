MiBeeNvr's H.265 WASM Live Playback: Honest Degradation Based on Decode Time

November 26, 2026 5 min read
Browser support for H.265 is patchy: WebRTC’s WHEP track cannot carry H.265 at all, HTTP-FLV’s mpegts.js cannot decode it, and HLS over MSE looks “supported” in quite a few browsers while silently dropping the fMP4 bytes and leaving the picture stuck at readyState=1 (HAVE_METADATA). Pick the wrong protocol for an H.265 camera and you get a black screen with a spinner. MiBeeNvr covers this path with a libde265 WASM software decoder: NAL units go into WASM, come out as YUV, are converted to RGBA, then render through Canvas2D or WebGL2/WebGPU. It does not need HTTPS — WebCodecs is undefined in a non-secure context — so plain HTTP can still play H.265. But software decoding moves the cost from the server to the viewer’s CPU: a 4K stream measured about 0.2fps against a 15–25fps source. Once decode falls behind, the backlog grows and the viewer ends up looking at green or white concealment blocks across the lower half of the frame.

MiBeeNvr's Lock-Free Camera Manager: From Seconds to Milliseconds at Cold Start

November 26, 2026 6 min read
The camera list was unusable for the first few minutes after boot: in a 9-camera deployment a single /api/cameras request took 14.9 seconds, then fell back to 1.4 seconds while continuing to stutter. The surveillance grid’s latest-frame polls stalled with it — during a camera reconnect, each tile refreshed only every few hundred milliseconds. The bottleneck was neither decoding nor the network. The camera manager had one coarse-grained cm.mu (sync.RWMutex), and the lifecycle path held it across slow I/O: Stop joins goroutines, startup dials and performs an ONVIF handshake, and saving configuration writes to disk. Any camera starting, stopping, or updating put every reader in a queue.
