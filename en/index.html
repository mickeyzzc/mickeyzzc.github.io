Four Protocol Libraries, Extracted: ONVIF and GB28181 in Go and Rust

August 29, 2026 8 min read
MiBee’s camera access runs on two protocols. ONVIF is the de facto standard internationally — Hikvision, Axis, Dahua, and Bosch all speak it. GB/T 28181 is China’s national surveillance standard, unavoidable in government and state-owned projects. The device side (Raspberry Pi camera firmware, one Go and one Rust implementation) and the NVR side (MiBeeNvr, Go) both speak these two protocols, and the stacks have always lived inside the projects. Two problems with that. First, this code is pickled in interop details — digest-auth qop handling, SIP Via-branch uniqueness, MANSCDP element-vs-attribute dual forms, TCP framing header byte counts — each one was ground out against a real platform, and a new project would have to grind them all again. Second, a library survey two weeks ago showed the open-source landscape for these protocols is unevenly populated; several positions have no one standing in them.

Taking Over onvif-go: Real-Camera Fixes, Zero Dependencies, and Service Facades

August 27, 2026 9 min read
MiBeeNvr talks to cameras over ONVIF, and this library is what sits underneath. ONVIF is the de facto standard for IP video surveillance equipment: device discovery, media profiles, PTZ control, event subscriptions — all one set of SOAP-over-HTTP service conventions. Any brand that claims ONVIF compliance can, in theory, be integrated through the same calls. onvif-go is that stack implemented in Go. We did not write it from scratch: its upstream was 0x524a/onvif-go — the previous author reshaped it into its modern form of a client plus a virtual camera server (the git history still traces back to earlier Go ONVIF attempts in the community). But upstream stalled entering 2026: the last merged PR landed in mid-January, the v1.0-through-v1.1.3 release cadence from late 2025 never resumed, and three Improve Test Coverage issues filed in November 2025 sit unclaimed to this day. Meanwhile we hit two problems in our own camera pool that could only be fixed inside the library.
