Storage Choices and Memory Budget on a 16 GiB Box

November 4, 2026 4 min read
The storage backend decides more than performance — it decides whether migration is a downtime event or a live one. That is the one sentence this post rests on. Along the way: the memory budget for a 16G box, because an uncapped ZFS ARC is a memory thief. The conclusion first incus move fundamentally means moving instance disks from A to B: If A and B share one storage (Ceph and friends), no data moves — only runtime state does → live migration, no downtime; With local disks on each node (my case), data is physically copied → downtime migration, seconds to tens of seconds for containers, longer for VMs. So the first storage question is not “which is fastest” but “am I willing to pay Ceph’s price for zero-downtime migration”. My answer: no. Two 16G boxes cannot carry Ceph’s minimum of 3 monitors + OSDs, and “no mutual replication” makes its replication useless here. Accept the short downtime window on local disks — that is the entire cost.

MiBeeBell: 30 Alerts In, One Notification Out

November 2, 2026 8 min read
One camera in my studio watches a hallway. Headlights sweeping past, a moth landing on the lens, tree shadows moving in the wind — all of it gets classified as motion detection. Dozens of alerts hit my phone in a night; once the push volume crosses a threshold I stop registering them, and the one alert that actually matters disappears into the noise. For years the alerting here was improvised: the NVR pushed its own notifications, Steward’s device alerts went out as SMTP mail, and IoT device anomalies were dropped into an MQTT topic nobody watched. Channels scattered, no aggregation, no silence windows, and nowhere to manage the rules as a whole. MiBeeBell is the alert notification center I wrote for exactly this: every system sends its events to it, it does the noise reduction, and only the part worth interrupting you reaches a channel you picked. It is a CGO-free single binary in Go with SQLite for storage — Raspberry-Pi-class hardware can run it as a resident service. The name joins the MiBee studio brand with “Bell” — the bell a hive rings when something is wrong.
