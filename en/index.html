onvif-c Tutorial: Adding an ONVIF Device to Your ESP32 Firmware

October 3, 2026 11 min read
onvif-c is the most “physical” of the five protocol libraries: a plain C ONVIF device component for ESP-IDF with a footprint around 10KB and zero third-party dependencies. When an ESP32 camera firmware needs to be discovered by an NVR, answer device-information queries, hand out stream URIs, and report motion events — this one component covers it. There is no XML parser inside: action recognition is strstr(), responses are assembled with snprintf(), and the memory model is simple enough to call safely from sensor callback context. The four MiBee camera firmware repos carry it in lockstep, and library changes go through real hardware before release.

Who Speaks, Who They Mean, Whether to Answer: Response Decision in Voice Dialogue

October 2, 2026 17 min read
The previous survey had a section on “how a conversation starts” comparing wake words with other entry points. This post goes one layer deeper: what does the wake word actually do for the system, and what has to replace it when you remove it? The answer is three judgments. When you call “Hey XiaoAi” at a speaker, the system instantly settles three things: whoever says the name is probably an enrolled user (who is speaking); the name is aimed at the device (who they’re talking to); a name obviously deserves a reply (whether to answer). Human conversation never starts every sentence with a name, because the human brain runs these three judgments continuously in the background — recognizing voices, working out whether a remark is aimed at you, deciding when to chime in and when to just listen.
